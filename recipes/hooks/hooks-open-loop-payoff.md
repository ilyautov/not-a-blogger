## When
The script explains the cause right away (no question left by second 6), or the hook promises one thing and the ending delivers a moral instead of an answer.

## Do
1. Ask one concrete question in the hook: "Why did the agent say the test passed when it fails?", "There are three catches, but first: how it works."
2. Every next shot adds evidence or raises the stakes. Cut restatements of what is already clear.
3. The ending pays exactly what was promised: a line of code, a test result, a before/after value. Not a slogan.
4. Phrase the conclusion as a shareable one-line definition.

## Verify
Write the hook question and the last sentence side by side: the second answers the first.

## Don't
- Don't stretch the loop in a short reel: a 139 s loop conflicts with the "reel ≤ 40 s" rule. Short version: hook, three catches one sentence each, punch.
- Don't end on a moral.

## Evidence
Analysed hit, 269k views: 0–3.4 s price and "an employee who never sleeps"; 3.4–6.5 s promise "three catches, but first how it works". Catches start at 86 s, so the answer is delayed almost 80 s; the punch is a definition at 130–134 s. Single hit, causality not proven.
