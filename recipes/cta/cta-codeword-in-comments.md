## When
"Subscribe" and "like" endings get skipped. Few comments, while comments drive reach and show who cares about the topic.

## Do
1. Last line: a short question about the video's topic + a codeword with a promise. Same question on an on-screen plate and in the caption.
2. One simple word tied to the topic: "keys", "voice", "ads".
3. A concrete, already prepared promise: cheat sheet, tool link, guide.
4. Reply to everyone who typed the word, but pace it: a cap per hour, not a burst. Prefer the platform's official messaging API (for Instagram: the Graph API with a business account) over a logged-in browser.
5. Vary the reply text; don't send the bare link as the first message. First a line of text ("here is the cheat sheet"), the link after, or point to a public page from the profile.
6. Never run DM replies from a browser profile that automation also drives (uploads, scraping).

```text
Which skill would you write first?
Comment "keys" and I'll send all the shortcuts on one page.
```

## Verify
Comments-to-views ratio is higher than for your videos without a word. Compare within one platform and one period.

## Don't
- Don't use the DM codeword on YouTube: there are no DMs, see [YouTube Shorts: profile link or Related Video](cta-youtube-link-in-description.md).
- Don't answer a wave of codewords by hand in one sitting with the same link: about 15 identical-link DM threads in 11 minutes preceded an account suspension (see Evidence; the cause is not confirmed).
- Don't compare videos by raw comment counts under a codeword: they inflate engagement; count unique external authors, see [analytics](../analytics/analytics-unique-external-authors.md).

## Evidence
Sample of 1,470 reels from 101 Russian-speaking AI creators over 30 days: 147 reels with a codeword in the caption had a median of 22 comments at a median of 1,105 views, comments/views 1.96 %; 1,323 without a word had a median of 6 comments, 0.26 %. A 7.5× difference. In another sample of viral reels a codeword brought 2.7k to 29k comments even to small accounts.

Ban risk, one case (n=1, cause not confirmed by the platform): a reel with a codeword collected 386 comments; the next evening about 15 DM threads with the same link went out by hand in 11 minutes from a browser profile also used by upload automation. The next morning the account was suspended. The DM bot connected to the account had sent nothing in three days; a read-only scan from the same IP (528 requests to public embeds) happened the same morning, so it can't be ruled out either. Treat the pacing rules above as a precaution, not a measured limit; no safe number per hour is known.
