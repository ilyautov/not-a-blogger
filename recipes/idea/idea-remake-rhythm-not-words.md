## When
Choosing what to build next, or you already have a hit you want to copy. Own-topic ideas have no proven format; a loose "inspired by" retelling loses the rhythm; re-uploading someone else's media gets flagged as non-original.

## Do
1. Pick a **fresh proven hit of your niche**: published recently, ≥ 5× its author's median (see [hits by median](idea-find-hits-by-median.md)). This is the working mode, not a fallback: remake proven hits, don't invent formats.
2. Break the hit down per second: contact sheets at fps=4, cuts via scene detection, loudness, transcript with word timestamps.
3. Transfer the skeleton, not the text: hook template, length, number of lines, positions of cuts, pauses, where the punch lands, the kind of visual device (split frame, screen recording, character, prop).
4. Tabulate the transcript: line number, length in words, role (hook, open loop, example, punch, CTA). Fill the same table with your **own lines written from your own material**: same roles and order, line length ±1 word if the line has ≤ 5 words, otherwise ±20 %. Do not translate or rephrase the original's lines or jokes, not even in another language: a translation is a derivative of their text. Story, example, numbers and names come from your own work or a source you checked.
5. Rebuild everything with your own means: your voice or TTS, your footage or renders, your captions. Improve with your own style where you can (character, 3D, better typography); keep the timing.
6. Check the longest shared word run with the original, ≤ 2 words and only function words. For a cross-language remake, also check that no line is a translation of an original line (read them side by side).

```bash
yt-dlp --skip-download --write-auto-subs --sub-langs <lang> --sub-format vtt -o orig "URL"
ffmpeg -i ref.mp4 -vf "fps=4,scale=270:-1,tile=8x6" contact_%02d.jpg      # 12 s per sheet
ffmpeg -i ref.mp4 -vf "select='gt(scene,0.3)',showinfo" -f null - 2>&1 | grep -o "pts_time:[0-9.]*" > cuts.txt
ffmpeg -i ref.mp4 -af ebur128 -f null - 2>&1 | tail -12 > loudness.txt
```

```python
import re
def longest_common_run(a: str, b: str) -> int:
    a, b = re.findall(r"\w+", a.lower()), re.findall(r"\w+", b.lower())
    best = 0
    for i in range(len(a)):
        for j in range(len(b)):
            k = 0
            while i + k < len(a) and j + k < len(b) and a[i + k] == b[j + k]:
                k += 1
            best = max(best, k)
    return best          # > 2 → rewrite the line
```

## Verify
- Role table matches the original line for line; cut count and total length within the original's. For a split frame (face bottom, video top), detect cuts on the top half separately.
- `longest_common_run` ≤ 2 for every line; no line is a translation or paraphrase of an original line.
- Not one frame, audio sample or face from the original is in the export: check your project's asset list (every file in the render's sources is yours, licensed, or generated by you). `ffmpeg -i` of the finished file cannot tell you where its frames came from.
- 24 h after publishing, compare with your account's median, not with the hit.

## Don't
- Don't re-upload someone else's video, audio, frames or faces, even cropped or sped up: platforms flag non-original uploads and the account loses reach.
- Don't commit frames or transcripts of other people's videos to a public repo; keep them in a local evidence folder.
- Don't remake on fictional data: rhythm doesn't save a video without a real story or result.
- Don't assume the format transfers across platforms.

## Evidence
- A video built on a hit's rhythm with its own lines: 212k Instagram views in one evening (21 Oct 2026), 21.5k likes and 257 comments by the next day; neighbouring reels of the same account had 58–400 views. The same video got 1 to 328 views on YouTube and TikTok. Four lines that echoed the original's constructions were rewritten before release. Status stays hypothesis: one video on one account does not show which part of it caused the views.
- Breakdown of seven hits: in every video with speech the first word lands at 0.0 s; none has an intro.
- Two remakes of business hits on fictional data ("example store") were rejected by the editor entirely.
- Working-mode observation (26 Oct 2026): on the same account, videos from own ideas had the account's usual tens to hundreds of views, and remakes of proven hits had its highest view counts. One account, no controlled comparison: the counts are facts, the claim that the remake method caused them is a hypothesis.
