## When
You need a timeless topic that works outside the AI news cycle, or the channel's AI videos sit at the median.

## Do
1. Pick an everyday thing whose origin hides a surprise ("why is a software error called a bug").
2. Frame 0: an archival photo of the object plus the question as the first line, within the first 0.2 s. No intro.
3. One object per frame; draw it in thin-line isometric or show the photo itself, nothing else competing.
4. A small "year · detail" badge on each archival shot.
5. Captions of 2–3 words, voice-over, no face needed.
6. Answer the riddle at the end and loop the last frame into the first.
7. Close with a share prompt ("send this to someone who…") instead of a subscription ask.
8. Every archival image gets source and license before it goes into the frame.

## Verify
- The first frame contains the photo and the first spoken words are the question: check frame 0 and the ASR of the first second.
- A contact sheet shows one object per frame and every archival shot has its badge.
- The asset list has a source and a license per photo.

## Don't
- Don't use photos without a checked license; see [image-archive-footage-prelinger](../image/image-archive-footage-prelinger.md) for one public-domain route.
- Don't stack several objects and a two-line caption into one frame: the format relies on one thing at a time.
- Don't present the format as proven: we have not published one ourselves.

## Evidence
From our snapshot of AI-niche reels (October 2026, public rounded counters): a "history of technology" series video got 443K views (×57) against a series median of 7.7K, while the same creator's AI videos stayed near the median. The grammar above (archival photo and question in the first 0.2 s, one object in thin-line isometric, 2–3-word captions, voice-over without a face, a share-prompt ending) comes from breaking that series down.
