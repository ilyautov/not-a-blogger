## When
A video took off and its comments are full of the same kind of question ("does this work on my brand of TV?", "and on the phone?") or a running joke about the video itself (a sound viewers mock).

## Do
1. Within a day of the spike, sort the top comments into: questions that extend the topic, jokes about the video's form, everything else.
2. Pick one extending question with the most likes and make a short answer video: the hook quotes the comment (on screen, as a comment card), the answer comes by 3–5 s.
3. Keep the voice and the look so viewers recognise the series, but build new scenes around the new object: a copy of a hit's skeleton with swapped words was called slop (see [no sequel on the same template](idea-no-repeat-own-hit.md)).
4. Keep the running joke: if viewers mock a sound or a detail, don't "fix" it in the answer video, it fuels comments (see [noise whooshes](../sound/sound-keep-noise-whooshes.md)).
5. Fact-check the answer: comment questions often hide a "no" (the trick works on one platform, not the other). Say the honest limit in the video.

## Verify
The answer video's hook shows the original comment within the first second; the fact-check file lists the platform limits you claimed.

## Don't
- Don't answer a question you can't verify on the real device or software.
- Don't wait a week: the topic cools down with the original video.

## Evidence
A video that collected 212K views in 9 hours had 257 comments; the top comment was a joke about a "sniffing" sound (111 likes), and several asked whether the trick works on another TV platform. An answer video on that question was built (honest limit: the other platform allows only an app, not a full replacement) but not published yet. No metric for the answer format: hypothesis.
