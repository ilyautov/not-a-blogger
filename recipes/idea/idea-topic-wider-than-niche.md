## When
Choosing a topic for a niche channel (AI tools, coding agents) and every candidate is "news about model X" or "feature Y of tool Z".

## Do
1. Start from a pain or story anyone has: ads on the TV, a cancelled call, a timeless "why does this work like that".
2. Add the niche as the twist: AI or the agent is how the pain got solved, not the subject.
3. Write the topic as one sentence for a non-specialist. If it needs a term from the niche to make sense, widen it.
4. Keep niche-only topics for long-forms and your own artifact; don't expect them to break out as shorts.

## Verify
Read the one-sentence topic to someone outside IT. They should be able to say why it matters to them without a follow-up question. Record their answer next to the idea.

## Don't
- Don't confuse with [idea-everyday-absurd](idea-everyday-absurd.md): that recipe is the script shape ("asked the AI → absurd result", genre worlds); this one is the topic filter that comes before any script.
- Don't drop the niche entirely: the twist has to be real and verifiable, or the video stops being yours.

## Evidence
- `research/findings.en.md` point 6: the biggest hits of niche AI creators are often not about AI (a timeless story, everyday absurdity, a confession); niche videos stay near the median.
- `research/formats.en.md` point 9: a "history of technology" riddle got 443K (×57) against a series median of 7.7K; the topic is wider than the niche.
- Own video [tv-no-ads](../../examples/tv-no-ads/README.md): "an ad on your own TV, an AI removed it" reached 212K Instagram views the same evening and 792K three days later, against 58–400 for the account's neighbouring reels; the breakdown names "the topic makes sense to someone outside IT" as the first reason. The same video got up to 328 views on YouTube and TikTok, so platform luck is part of it. One own hit plus correlations: `hypothesis`.
