# "Asked an AI to free up my Friday"

![Frame from the video](poster.jpg)

YouTube Shorts: https://youtube.com/shorts/sNg2qsj1Ag4 (released 10.09.2026).

## Format and length

Vertical short, 1080×1920, 20 s, looped. Animated infographic in HTML/GSAP: day cards in a
weekly calendar. No face, synthesized voice. Genre: "asked the AI to do X, got absurdity".

## Hook (first line)

«Попросил нейросеть освободить пятницу.» [Asked an AI to free up my Friday.]

## Second-by-second structure

| Time | What you hear | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0.25–3.1 | «Попросил нейросеть освободить пятницу.» [Asked an AI to free up my Friday.] | Title "Free up Friday", five day cards, counter "meetings this week: 15" |
| 3.7–6.2 | «Только пятницу.» [Just Friday.] | A pusher knocks Friday off, and the other days start falling after it |
| 6.8–10.6 | (pause, sound of falling) | Cards topple one after another, the counter melts 15 → 12 → 6 → 0 |
| 10.6–12.6 | «Теперь свободна вся неделя.» [Now the whole week is free.] | Empty calendar, big "0 meetings", a checkmark |
| 13.0–17.1 | «Поручая нейросети задачу, скажи, что должно уцелеть.» [When you give an AI a task, say what has to survive.] | Conclusion in big text, the word «уцелеть» [survive] in red |
| 18.4–20.0 | (no words) | A "Restore all" button, the cards come back, the video loops to the start |

## Techniques used

- [Keep a reel under 40 seconds, one example, punchline before methodology](../../recipes/idea/idea-one-example-short-reel.md): four short lines (request, constraint, consequence, conclusion), 20 seconds.
- [Build a video from an everyday situation or genre parody, not from the tool](../../recipes/idea/idea-everyday-absurd.md): the absurdity is shown through action, not words: the days physically fall, the counter melts.
- A 4-second pause without speech while the picture does the work (no recipe yet).
- One counter running through the whole video as a measure of the disaster (no recipe yet).
- The conclusion is phrased as a tip you can apply right away (no recipe yet).
- Loop: the last second returns to the first frame, so people watch it a second time (no recipe yet).
- [Fix stress by rewording, not with marks, and synthesize in chunks](../../recipes/voice/voice-stress-by-rewording.md): the word «ИИ» [AI] was replaced with «нейросеть» [neural network], because the synthesizer mumbled the abbreviation.

## What worked

- An everyday situation familiar to anyone who works with a calendar.
- Understandable without sound: the falling days and the counter tell the story by themselves.
- A short looped video: 20 seconds is easy to finish and rewatch.
- The "asked the AI for X → absurdity" format turned out to be the channel's best by YouTube
  views; the next video in this formula is "Goro cancelled the call".

## What didn't work

- A ceiling of about 1.3K views: the video never left the test pool.
- It wasn't uploaded to Instagram, so the platforms can't be compared.
- The conclusion sounds like a moral. For a reel that's more of a plus, but this kind of ending
  doesn't invite comments: there's no question for the viewer.

## How to repeat it

1. Take a real-life request to an AI and push its literal execution to absurdity.
2. A 3–4-line script: request, clarification, disaster, conclusion.
3. Choose one visual metaphor (calendar, budget, letter) and one counter.
4. Let the picture work without the voice for 2–4 seconds.
5. Loop it: the last frame should flow into the first.
6. Add a question for the viewer at the end so comments show up.
