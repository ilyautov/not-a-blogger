# Formats that take off, and ones that don't

Conclusions from snapshots of Russian- and English-language reels and shorts about AI for September–October 2026:
hundreds of videos, selected by their multiple of the creator's median (see [findings.en.md](findings.en.md), point 1).
The numbers come from public rounded counters, with no completion data, so everything below is correlation, not an
experiment. Creator names are deliberately removed.

A format lives for about a week: see point 9 in [findings.en.md](findings.en.md). Take the structure, not the storyline.

1. **Skit dialogue "human and AI agent"**: a pain question → a deadpan AI reply that flips the blame → absurdity escalating in 3–4 steps → a useful nugget → punchline → an in-character CTA/meta twist. 45–67 s, cuts every 2–3 s, a dark stage with no chrome. Up to ×202 on likes, 4 hits in 4 days for one account.
2. **"Asked the AI → absurd result"** (iterations of "make it even more…", character comedy about agents, a staff of agents). 7.8M (×8,562) in Russian, 285K for an account with 3.9K followers.
3. **"The agent was shown an ordinary thing → something impossible → it concerns you → it's free, install X"** (an open-source tool turns your real world into something new). ×258–681, 1.6M; fades within 3–4 days.
4. **Fear of being exposed + proof with a detector** ("your text gets flagged as AI", 93% → 0%). 430K ×34; 3 hits at ×9–96 in a month + 583K on YouTube; a tutorial for the same tool: 43 views.
5. **An expert versus someone else's viral claim**: a hook with the other person's clip and subtitles, the answer at 3–7 s, ping-pong, proof on a live device, a signature closing line. Median 311K with a flat distribution; hits stand out by ×1.4 comments.
6. **"An AI employee" with a price or arithmetic** ("$N a month: an employee who never sleeps, there are three catches"; "20 × 10 min = 70 h/month"), with a business task inside as the example. 269K ×72; ×33 for an account with 1.7K followers.
7. **A prompt for a specific niche → a finished result on a phone screen, no voice** (10–30 s, the code word inside the result). 7 hits in a month at ×12–18 for one account; AI in spreadsheets ×88.
8. **A mechanism in motion: "let's follow one order/request", "how X works inside"** (blueprint lines, one accent, "step N/M", each line its own movement). 967K ×228; 447K on an account's 11th post.
9. **A timeless story with a riddle question** (history of technology: an archival photo + a question in the hook, one object per frame, a "year · detail" badge, a looped ending). 443K (×57) against a series median of 7.7K; the topic is wider than the niche.
10. **The generative "bad picture" genre and live situations of a person with AI**: CCTV/doorbell cam, selfie vlog, AI satire where "the voice sells, the picture refutes", trolling an AI recruiter ("the machine can't break character"), "harmful" automation. 200M+ (CCTV), 863K ×638 (satire), 729K ×729 (recruiter).
11. **A tweet-style card on a looping b-roll**: 7–8 s, no voice, one line in bold from frame 0, below it something to copy; the background loops, the format counts on rewatches (the card can't be read in one pass). The reference creator's hits are on Instagram; elsewhere the ceiling is ~3K. Our version is built, not published: hypothesis. Loop: [image-seamless-loop-bg](../recipes/image/image-seamless-loop-bg.md).

Cross-cutting modifiers that amplify any format: a code word for a specific free thing; the first word at 0.0 s with no intro; a live narrator's voice; a poll question or a camps-arguing ending.

Anti-formats (don't take off): a tool tutorial without a situation, a narrow business function as the topic, a digest/retelling of news or a course with static visuals, frequent tests and comparisons, a one-off copy of an alarming headline without a reputation, sequels of a hit on the same storyline.

## Own observations outside the sample

- **Period styling instead of "neural" realism**: a 1930s rubber-hose cartoon (canvas 2D, a gag on every line) and a 1950s educational film (generated video + Prelinger archive footage + a 78rpm march, see [image-archive-footage-prelinger](../recipes/image/image-archive-footage-prelinger.md)). The channel owner loved both, but in live upload slots their views matched other styles (YouTube 972 and 1,146 against 1,067 for a neighbour the same day), not more. Styling, not the cause of reach: hypothesis.
